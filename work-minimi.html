From 10 paying users a month to 850+: how one ICP shift rebuilt Minimi

A product and growth internship story about finding the market that actually wanted the product.

Minimi is a context layer on your Mac screen. It remembers what you are working on so your AI does not force you to re-explain yourself every session.

Before the shift, it was shipped as Shram — a PM/context tool built around project and ticket context for teams. It was pulling in about 10 new paying users a month, with churn around 30%.

There was no real global pull, and churn was eating growth faster than outbound could replace it. The product was not failing because it had no features; it was failing because it was solving the wrong job for the wrong people.

Why it mattered: we were in a narrow launch window and had no time to keep optimizing around a weak buyer. This was a product-market fit problem, not a marketing problem.

I ran ongoing calls alongside growth across existing users and new prospects: CEOs, PMs, ops leads, GTM leaders and developers. The assumed ICP was teams needing a shared PM/context tool.

The pattern: people were not asking for a better project tool. They were asking for AI that already knew what was on their screen.

01
Rebrand: Shram → Minimi. “Shram” framed work management. The new ICP was about memory and context, so the name, narrative and positioning moved with it. I owned the narrative and led the reframe.
02
Onboarding rebuild: before: workspace setup → add projects/tickets → invite team → value only after team setup. after: install Mac app → grant screen access → first AI answer already has your context. Value moved from “after setup” to “first session.”
03
Feature shipped: a closed-loop recommendation flow that detects intent mid-conversation, fills the cart and places the order with user permission. It was designed around the exact user pain: AI already has the context, so it can act before the user re-explains.
04
Tradeoff: we dropped the team/PM use case entirely and went all in on the individual. Smaller deal size, faster activation and a much stronger retention curve.

Paying users grew from 10 a month to 850+ over one summer. The new ICP made the pitch land on the first try, contributing to a 57% onboarded-to-paid rate. Moving value into the first session helped reduce churn from 30% to 15%.

The rebrand and new narrative supported two Product Hunt launches at #2 with zero paid spend, while global-first positioning brought users from 25+ countries. I owned the ICP research, narrative, onboarding work and 1,500+ onboardings; the founders made the final rebrand call based on that research.

Next bet: self-serve onboarding. 1,500 manual onboardings do not scale past 10k.
Metric to watch: week-4 retention by segment, especially solo vs team users.
Mistake to fix: we were not instrumenting activation early enough, so before/after activation is still hard to prove. That needs to be tracked from day 1.
